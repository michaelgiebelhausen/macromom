# Product Vision — MacroMom

## 1. Vision & Mission

### Vision Statement

Every college student walks into their dining hall knowing exactly what to eat to become the healthiest, fittest version of themselves — without a nutrition degree, a personal coach, or a single moment of guesswork.

### Mission Statement

MacroMom turns each campus dining hall's live daily menu into a personalized, goal-based meal plan delivered in the warm, encouraging voice of a mom who happens to be a certified macro-nutrition consultant.

### Founder's Why

MacroMom started at a kitchen table, not a whiteboard. Mike's son is heading to Clemson in the fall of 2026, and like every parent sending a kid off to a dining-hall meal plan, Mike knew exactly how that story usually goes: unlimited swipes, a wall of stations, and an eighteen-year-old with no framework for what to actually put on the plate. The freshman fifteen isn't a joke — it's a design failure of the environment. Nobody hands students the one thing they need: a translation of that overwhelming menu into "here's what to eat today."

Mike's wife, Alyssa, is a certified macro-nutrition consultant. She has the exact expertise the situation calls for — the methodology to take a person's goals and turn them into concrete macro targets, and the judgment to look at a plate and know whether it works. The gap was never knowledge; it was distribution. Alyssa can't sit in every dining hall in America, but her methodology can live in an app. That's the insight that makes MacroMom more than another calorie tracker: it's a way to put a real consultant's brain — and a mom's care — into a student's pocket at the exact moment of decision.

Mike is non-technical and building with AI, which is a feature, not a bug, for this product. It forces ruthless focus on the one thing that matters: the student opening the app and instantly knowing what to eat. Everything else is secondary. The founder-market fit here is unusually tight — a credentialed domain expert, a real first user (his own son), a captive beachhead (Clemson Greek life), and a hard, motivating deadline (move-in).

### Core Values

- **Zero cognitive load, always.** The student should never have to think. MacroMom does the analysis and hands back a decision, not a dashboard. Any feature that adds a step the student has to figure out is suspect until proven otherwise.
- **Credentialed, not crowd-sourced.** Recommendations trace back to Alyssa's real methodology, not internet trends or a generic algorithm. When the app says "eat this," it's because a certified consultant's logic says so. This is the trust moat — protect it.
- **Teach by osmosis, never by lecture.** Students learn nutrition as a byproduct of good recommendations with a one-line "why," never through lessons, quizzes, or content they didn't ask for. If it feels like homework, cut it.
- **Warm, never nagging.** MacroMom is the cheerleader mom who's proud of you, not the app that guilt-trips you for a bad day. Encouragement over enforcement, every time.
- **Real menus or nothing.** The product's whole reason to exist is that it knows what's actually being served today. Guessing from a generic food database would betray the core promise. Menu accuracy is sacred.

### Strategic Pillars

- **The magic moment comes first.** "Open app → here's what to eat at your hall today" must work flawlessly before anything else gets built. Every roadmap decision bends to protecting the shortest path to that moment.
- **Win one campus completely before winning many.** Clemson is the entire world until it's conclusively working. Depth over breadth — a few hundred delighted Clemson students beats a thin presence on ten campuses.
- **Proactive beats passive.** MacroMom reaches out to the student ("here's your game plan today") rather than waiting to be opened. The notification is the product as much as the app is.
- **Build for the beachhead's voice.** The tone, examples, and design should feel native to health-minded students — sorority women and guys who lift — not to their parents or to a clinical audience.

### Success Looks Like

It's early 2027. MacroMom has a few hundred paying students at Clemson, and it spread the way things spread in Greek life — one sorority told another, a couple of lifting guys posted their progress, and the app became a small campus norm. Mike's son uses it every day without being asked. Alyssa's methodology, encoded once, is now delivering thousands of personalized meal plans a month. The dining-hall scraping has been generalized past Clemson's specific menu source, so adding the next five to ten campuses is a config change, not a rebuild. Retention is strong because the app does one thing extremely well and never wastes the student's time. Mike has a real business with paying users, a defensible data-and-methodology moat, and a clear, un-hyped path to the next dozen campuses.

## 2. User Research

### Primary Persona

**Bailey, 19, sophomore at Clemson, Kappa sorority member.** She's on the unlimited residential meal plan and eats two to three times a day at the dining halls. She works out four or five times a week — a mix of lifting and Pilates — and genuinely cares about how she looks and feels, especially with formals, game days, and spring break always on the calendar. She's on her phone constantly (iPhone, of course) and lives in group chats. Her nutrition knowledge is a patchwork of TikTok advice: she knows "protein good," vaguely fears carbs, and has tried under-eating salads for a week only to crash and binge. She's tried MyFitnessPal twice and quit both times within days — scanning and searching every item is exhausting and the generic database never matches what the dining hall actually serves. Emotionally, she's frustrated and a little anxious: she's putting in gym work but feels like her eating is undoing it, and she doesn't trust her own choices. What would make her switch: something that just tells her what to grab, is dead simple, and clearly knows what she's talking about. If it felt like a smart, caring friend rather than a spreadsheet, she'd use it every day — and tell her whole sorority.

### Secondary Personas

- **Tyler, 20, lifts five days a week.** Already macro-aware and tracks loosely, but hates that no tool knows his dining hall's menu. He wants the data made usable — "just show me the high-protein options at my hall today." Not in Greek life; found the app through a gym friend. High-intent, low-hand-holding user.
- **The athlete (e.g. a D1 or club athlete).** Has performance goals and possibly guidance from a team, but still eats in the general dining halls and wants day-to-day help hitting targets. Values the menu-awareness most; least in need of the "mom" tone.
- **The parent (Mike, and parents like him).** Doesn't use the app daily but is the peace-of-mind buyer and word-of-mouth engine. May discover it, recommend it, or gift a subscription. Cares that their kid is eating well and that the guidance is credible.

### Jobs To Be Done

- **Functional:** "When I walk into the dining hall, help me decide what to put on my plate so I hit my goals without having to research or calculate anything."
- **Functional:** "When I've plated my food, tell me if my portions are roughly right so I'm not over- or under-eating."
- **Emotional:** "Help me feel in control of my eating and confident I'm not sabotaging my gym work."
- **Emotional:** "Make me feel looked-after and encouraged, not judged, on the days I don't eat perfectly."
- **Social:** "Let me be the friend who has her nutrition figured out — and who can recommend the thing that actually works."

### Pain Points

1. **Decision paralysis at the point of eating (high severity, 2–3× daily).** Faced with every station open, students default to comfort food or a random "healthy-looking" choice. The consequence compounds meal after meal — this is the core, highest-frequency pain and the one MacroMom exists to kill.
2. **No campus-specific data (high severity, constant).** Every existing tool assumes you know what you're eating and can find it in a generic database. Students are left translating a dining-hall menu into macros by hand, which nobody sustains. Currently they just guess.
3. **Guessing wrong in the name of "healthy" (high severity, frequent).** Under-eating, sad salads, all-protein fads — students hurt their own progress with well-intentioned but uninformed choices. The consequence is real: stalled results, low energy, crash-and-binge cycles.
4. **Tracking fatigue (medium-high severity, kills adoption).** Manual logging is so tedious that students abandon it within a week — so even motivated students end up with no tool at all.
5. **Portion misjudgment (medium severity, daily).** Even with the right foods, students routinely misjudge how much they've served themselves. Minor per meal, but it quietly undermines otherwise-good choices.

### Current Alternatives & Competitive Landscape

- **MyFitnessPal / Cronometer / other trackers.** Do comprehensive logging and have huge food databases. But they're built around manual data entry against a _generic_ database, know nothing about a specific dining hall's daily menu, and are passive — they wait for you to do the work. Switching to MacroMom means trading a data-entry chore for a proactive recommendation. Their strength (breadth) is irrelevant to a student who just needs today's dining-hall answer.
- **TikTok / Instagram nutrition advice.** Free, entertaining, and where the audience already is. But it's generic, often wrong, trend-driven, and never accounts for what's actually available at the student's hall. It creates the "guessing wrong" problem more than it solves it.
- **A human nutrition coach (like Alyssa).** The gold standard for personalization and trust — but costs hundreds a month, isn't campus-aware, and doesn't scale. MacroMom is explicitly the affordable, always-on, menu-aware embodiment of this.
- **Texting a parent "is this healthy?"** Emotionally resonant (and the exact behavior MacroMom productizes) but low-information, intermittent, and dependent on the parent's own knowledge.
- **Doing nothing / eating on vibes.** The real default and the biggest competitor. Frictionless but produces exactly the outcomes students fear. MacroMom has to be nearly as frictionless as doing nothing to win here — which is why zero cognitive load is a core value.

### Key Assumptions to Validate

- **We assume Clemson's dining-hall menus are reliably available online in a scrapeable, per-day, per-hall form** (likely via a vendor like Nutrislice or Dine On Campus). _To validate:_ before any real build, confirm Clemson's exact menu source and whether it exposes structured data (JSON) or requires HTML scraping. This is the single biggest technical risk — validate it first.
- **We assume the published menu data includes enough nutrition detail (macros per item) to build accurate recommendations.** _To validate:_ inspect actual Clemson menu data for calorie/protein/carb/fat fields. If missing, plan for an estimation layer (AI-assisted) and set expectations accordingly.
- **We assume students will pay ~$7/month for this.** _To validate:_ price-test with the first sorority cohort; watch trial-to-paid conversion. Students are price-sensitive — the free trial and clear value in week one are critical.
- **We assume proactive notifications drive the habit** (rather than annoying students into disabling them). _To validate:_ measure notification opt-in and daily-open rates; A/B the tone and timing.
- **We assume the "mom" persona delights rather than feels gimmicky to this demographic.** _To validate:_ qualitative feedback from the first cohort; watch whether students quote/screenshot the voice (a great sign) or find it cringe.
- **We assume portion-check-by-photo is good enough to be trusted.** _To validate:_ test AI portion estimates against known plates; if accuracy is shaky, position it as a rough "gut-check," not a precise measurement — and consider deferring it if it undermines trust.
- **We assume one campus's scraper generalizes to others without a rebuild.** _To validate:_ after Clemson works, survey the menu sources of the next few target campuses to confirm they share a vendor/format.

### User Journey Map

**Awareness:** Bailey hears about MacroMom from a sorority sister who won't stop talking about it, or sees a classmate's before/after. The pitch is one sentence: "it tells you what to eat at the dining hall." Emotion: curious, slightly skeptical she's heard "just track your food" before.

**Consideration:** She checks it out because it's specifically about _her_ dining halls at Clemson — not a generic app. That specificity is what overcomes her tracker-fatigue skepticism. Emotion: cautious optimism.

**First use / onboarding:** She sets a goal (e.g. "get lean and toned"), answers a few quick questions, and gets her personalized macro targets — explained in one warm sentence, not a wall of numbers. Friction risk: onboarding must be fast and feel like a caring intake, not a form. Emotion: "okay, this gets me."

**Magic moment:** The next morning, MacroMom messages her: "Morning! Here are three easy plates at [her hall] today that'll hit your goals." She taps one, walks in, plates it, snaps a photo to check her portion, and gets a cheerful "that's perfect." Emotion: relief and delight — for the first time, eating well was effortless.

**Habit formation:** The daily notification becomes part of her routine, like checking the weather. She trusts it, so she stops overthinking food. She starts absorbing the "why" without trying. Emotion: confidence, control.

**Advocacy:** She hits a visible result — feels leaner, has more energy, gets a compliment — and credits the app. She screenshots a funny/sweet MacroMom message into the group chat. The sorority network does the rest. Emotion: pride, ownership — "I found this."

## 3. Product Strategy

### Product Principles

- **A decision, not a dashboard.** Every core interaction ends with the student knowing what to do next. Data and charts are secondary to the recommendation.
- **The notification is the product.** Design the proactive daily message as a first-class surface, not an afterthought. If the app only worked through notifications and a quick tap, it should still deliver the magic moment.
- **Menu truth above all.** Never recommend food that isn't actually being served today. When menu data is uncertain, be honest rather than confidently wrong.
- **The voice is the moat.** Alyssa's methodology and the cheerleader-mom tone are what competitors can't copy quickly. Invest in getting the voice exactly right; treat it as a core feature, not copywriting.
- **Frictionless enough to beat "doing nothing."** Benchmark every flow against the effort of not using the app at all. If a step isn't worth the student's tap, remove it.
- **Ship the beachhead, not the vision.** Build for Clemson's specific halls and students first. Generalization comes only after depth is proven.

### Market Differentiation

Every nutrition app on Bailey's phone shares one fatal assumption: that she knows what she's about to eat and is willing to log it. MacroMom throws that assumption out. It starts from the one piece of information no competitor uses — _what her specific dining hall is actually serving today_ — and works backward to a decision she can act on in seconds. That's a structural advantage, not a feature: MyFitnessPal can add a "dining hall mode," but they'd have to rebuild around campus menu data and abandon their generic-database, manual-logging DNA to do it. Layered on top is a second moat that's even harder to copy: a real certified consultant's methodology delivered in a voice students actually enjoy. Generic apps have algorithms; MacroMom has Alyssa. The combination — real menus + real methodology + a mom who cares — is defensible precisely because it's not a feature bolt-on; it's the whole architecture. And it compounds: each campus's structured menu data and each refinement of the recommendation logic makes the next campus and the next student better served.

### Magic Moment Design

The magic moment is: **MacroMom proactively messages the student with a few ready-to-plate meal options for today at their dining hall; they walk in, plate it, and snap a photo to check portions — no thinking required.**

For this to happen reliably, several things must be true in the MVP:

- The student's dining hall(s) and goal must be captured in onboarding, so the app knows _whose_ plan and _which_ menu.
- Today's menu for that hall must be scraped, current, and have enough nutrition detail to match against macro targets.
- The recommendation engine must reliably produce 2–3 sensible, appealing plates from that menu that fit the student's targets.
- The proactive notification must fire at the right time (before a meal) and deep-link straight into the recommendation.
- The portion-check camera must give a fast, trustworthy gut-check.

The shortest path from sign-up to magic moment: onboarding (goal + hall) → first daily recommendation. That should be achievable within a day of signing up, and ideally the app can show a sample "today's plate" _during_ onboarding so the student feels the value before the first notification even arrives. **This magic moment is achievable in the MVP** — it is the MVP. The portion-camera is the one piece that could be simplified or fast-followed if AI accuracy proves shaky, without breaking the core moment.

### MVP Definition

In scope for v1 — buildable in a focused ~4–8 week window with an AI coding agent:

- **Goal-based onboarding + macro targets.** Capture the student's goal and basic stats; calculate personalized macro targets via Alyssa's methodology. Done when a new user finishes onboarding with targets set. Essential — nothing personalizes without it.
- **Dining-hall menu ingestion for Clemson.** A scheduled scraper that pulls today's menu per hall into structured data. Done when Clemson halls reliably have current daily menus in the database. Essential — this is the core data asset.
- **Daily recommendation engine.** Given today's menu + the student's targets, produce 2–3 plate recommendations with per-plate macros. Done when a student can see appealing, goal-fitting plates from real menu data. Essential — this is the magic moment.
- **Proactive daily notification.** Push a warm, on-brand message before meals that deep-links to today's recommendations. Done when students reliably receive and can tap through. Essential — proactivity is the differentiator.
- **Portion-check camera.** Photograph a plate; get a fast portion gut-check. Done when a student gets sensible feedback on a photo. Essential to the stated magic moment, but the simplest acceptable version is fine for v1.
- **Basic logging + progress.** Let students confirm what they ate and see simple progress toward goals. Done when a student can look back at the week. Essential enough to prove value and retention.
- **Auth + subscription gating.** Sign in with Apple/email; free trial then paid subscription via IAP. Done when a user can sign up, trial, and be gated to paid. Essential for the business model.

### Explicitly Out of Scope

- **Android app.** Tempting because "guys who lift" skew Android — but the beachhead (sorority women) is heavily iPhone, and Expo lets us add Android later cheaply. _Reconsider:_ after Clemson is proven, when expanding segments.
- **Multiple campuses.** Tempting for TAM, but generalizing before one campus works risks doing everything poorly. _Reconsider:_ at the 6-month mark, once scraping is generalized.
- **Overt educational content (courses, articles, quizzes).** Tempting because nutrition education is valuable — but the founder explicitly wants learning to be a byproduct, and content is a maintenance burden. _Reconsider:_ only if users ask for deeper "why."
- **Social / friend features (leaderboards, sharing challenges).** Tempting given the Greek-life network — but it's a distraction from the core loop for v1. _Reconsider:_ post-launch, as a growth lever once retention is proven.
- **Meal-plan history analytics / deep dashboards.** Tempting for power users — but it violates "a decision, not a dashboard." Keep progress simple. _Reconsider:_ if engaged users request it.
- **Web app + Stripe checkout.** Tempting for parents-as-buyers and to avoid Apple's cut — but it's a second surface. _Reconsider:_ once mobile is working, as a gifting/parent flow.
- **Off-campus / restaurant / grocery recommendations.** Tempting for "all day" coverage — but it dilutes the dining-hall focus that makes the app special. _Reconsider:_ well after core success.

### Feature Priority (MoSCoW)

- **Must Have:** Goal onboarding + macro targets; Clemson menu ingestion; daily recommendation engine; proactive daily notification; auth; subscription gating with free trial.
- **Should Have:** Portion-check camera; basic logging; simple progress view; the "why" one-liner in recommendations.
- **Could Have:** Manual food add/override; multiple-hall switching in a single day; adjustable notification timing; streaks/encouragement milestones.
- **Won't Have (this time):** Android; multiple campuses; social features; educational content library; deep analytics dashboards; web/Stripe flow; off-campus recommendations.

### Core User Flows

**Flow 1 — Onboarding to first targets.** Trigger: new user opens app. Steps: sign in with Apple → select goal → enter basic stats → select home dining hall(s) → see personalized macro targets explained in one warm sentence → (bonus) see a sample plate from today's menu. Outcome: user has targets and a hall set. Success criteria: user reaches a set-targets state in under ~3 minutes and sees at least one real menu-based example.

**Flow 2 — The daily magic moment.** Trigger: proactive morning notification. Steps: tap notification → land on today's 2–3 recommended plates for their hall, each with macros and a one-line "why" → pick one → (at the hall) plate it → snap a photo → get portion feedback → confirm/log. Outcome: student eats a goal-fitting meal effortlessly. Success criteria: from notification tap to a chosen plate in under ~20 seconds; portion feedback returns in a few seconds.

**Flow 3 — Progress check.** Trigger: student opens app to see how they're doing. Steps: open app → view simple weekly progress toward goals with an encouraging summary. Outcome: student feels in control and motivated. Success criteria: the view loads instantly and leads with encouragement, not raw numbers.

### Success Metrics

- **Primary metric:** Daily active recommendation usage — the share of subscribed students who tap through to a recommendation on a given day. This is the magic moment happening. _Good:_ 40% of subscribers daily. _Great:_ 60%+.
- **Secondary metrics:** Trial-to-paid conversion (_good:_ 25%, _great:_ 40%+); 4-week retention (_good:_ 40%, _great:_ 60%+); notification opt-in rate (_good:_ 60%, _great:_ 80%+).
- **Leading indicators:** Onboarding completion rate; time-to-first-recommendation; number of Clemson halls with reliably current menus; qualitative "would you tell a friend?" from the first cohort.

### Risks

- **Menu data availability/accuracy (high likelihood, high impact).** If Clemson's menus aren't scrapeable or lack nutrition detail, the core promise breaks. _Mitigation:_ validate the exact data source before building anything else; build an AI estimation fallback for missing macros; be transparent when data is uncertain.
- **Timeline vs. scope (high likelihood, medium impact).** One month is aggressive for a native app with scraping and vision. _Mitigation:_ protect the magic moment; treat portion-camera and polish as fast-follows that can slip past move-in without breaking the core.
- **Portion-photo accuracy (medium likelihood, medium impact).** If AI portion estimates are unreliable, they could erode trust. _Mitigation:_ position as a gut-check, not a scale; test against known plates; be willing to defer.
- **Retention / novelty wear-off (medium likelihood, high impact).** Students may try it and drift. _Mitigation:_ proactive notifications as habit anchor; keep the loop effortless; visible progress and encouragement.
- **Price sensitivity (medium likelihood, medium impact).** Students are broke. _Mitigation:_ free trial that delivers value in week one; annual plan for committed users; keep monthly modest (~$7).
- **Apple App Store approval (medium likelihood, medium impact).** IAP rules, health-adjacent content, data handling. _Mitigation:_ use RevenueCat + IAP correctly from the start; clear privacy handling; avoid medical claims.
- **Single-champion dependence (low-medium likelihood, medium impact).** Early growth leans on one sorority network. _Mitigation:_ seed multiple pockets (a sorority + a lifting friend group); make sharing natural.
- **Founder is non-technical (low likelihood given AI tooling, medium impact).** Debugging or infra hiccups could stall progress. _Mitigation:_ managed services (Supabase, RevenueCat, OpenRouter) that minimize ops; a roadmap written for AI-agent execution; PR review workflow to catch issues.

## 4. Brand Strategy

### Positioning Statement

For health-minded college students who want to eat well and get fit but don't know nutrition, MacroMom is the dining-hall nutrition app that tells you exactly what to eat today — in the caring voice of a mom who's a certified macro consultant. Unlike generic trackers that make you log a generic database by hand, MacroMom starts from your actual dining hall's live menu and just hands you the plan.

### Brand Personality

MacroMom is your mom, if your mom were also a certified macro-nutrition consultant and your biggest cheerleader. She's warm, upbeat, and genuinely proud of you. She talks to you like she believes in you — because she does. She's knowledgeable but never shows off; she gives you the answer and maybe one quick "here's why," never a lecture. She notices your wins ("three days hitting your protein — that's my girl") and she never, ever guilt-trips you for a pizza day ("no worries, we've got tomorrow"). If she were a person, she'd text you a heart emoji sparingly but a genuine "so proud of you" often. She'd wear something comfortable and put-together. She would never nag, never shame, never bury you in numbers, and never pretend to be a doctor. She's the calm, capable, loving presence that makes a big scary thing (eating right, alone, for the first time) feel handled.

### Voice & Tone Guide

**Voice (always):** Warm, encouraging, clear, and credible. Cheerful without being saccharine. Restrained with emojis. Plain language a 19-year-old uses, but grounded in real expertise. Leads with the person, backs it with the numbers.

### Messaging Framework

  3. _Zero effort, all support._ No tedious logging. Just a caring daily nudge and a plan that takes the thinking off your plate.

### Competitive Differentiation Narrative

The nutrition app market is enormous and, from a student's point of view, completely useless at the moment that matters. MyFitnessPal and its peers are logging tools built on generic food databases — they assume you already know what you're eating and are willing to type it in. No student sustains that, and none of these tools know a single thing about what's actually being served at a specific college dining hall tonight. TikTok fills the vacuum with confident, contradictory, trend-driven advice that makes the guessing worse. And an actual nutrition consultant — the only thing that truly works — costs hundreds a month and can't sit in the dining hall with you. MacroMom occupies the empty middle: it's the only product that starts from the student's real, daily menu and applies a certified consultant's methodology to hand back a decision, proactively, in a voice students actually enjoy. That's not a feature competitors can quickly bolt on; it's a different architecture — campus menu data plus real methodology plus a beloved persona — and every campus we structure and every recommendation we refine widens the moat. We're not building a better tracker. We're building the thing students actually wanted the whole time: someone who just tells them what to eat.
